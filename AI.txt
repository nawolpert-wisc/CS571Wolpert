AI was used for essentially 100% of the website. But I used a combination of Figma's prototyping logics, copilot and claude.

Through this I found Figma to give the most aesthetically pleasing website, but difficult to pull into a JS level project.
The next step for me was to use copilot to try and adapt the original figma files to something viewable in gitlab-pages.

For the Figma prompt I used a modified version of the submission website proposal where I went more in depth and used the low
fidelity prototype to help describe the flow of the webpage.

However, turned out copliot was not great at adapthing these files so I spent the better part of 3 hours just trying to 
the styling of the webpage so that it was not just an unreadable jumble of information. Originally I thought it was an issue 
with the card build and how that might be causing weird interactions with how items were divided with in the actual space of 
webpage. But that wasn't the issue.

This eventually made it so I had to have claude update the CSS with tailwind CSS as that is what figma uses for their prototyping
which did cause quite a bit of frustration on trying to get the correct vite.config to be used as it was pulling a secondary
file that was generated as part of the boilerplate app dev from the first assignment.

After that I went on to try and correct the weird AI artifacts with in the webpage itself, i.e. random pictures of essentially
getysburg images of people.

Updates made:
The map now supports click-to-pin: clicking a marker (or a row in the "Key Locations" list) locks its description in the 
detail panel until you click a different marker/row or click elsewhere, instead of the description vanishing the moment you 
moved the mouse off. Markers still show a quick preview on hover, and a browser-default focus outline that appeared on click was 
suppressed in favor of the app's own circle highlight. The list initially got the same hover-preview treatment as the markers, but 
that caused hovering to get stuck — since hovering a row swapped it out for the detail view, the row's own mouse-leave never fired. 
That was resolved by dropping hover from the list entirely, leaving it click-only while the markers keep both hover and click.
